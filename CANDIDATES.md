# Candidates — Agent Motto Battle (u-febri-yudila-agent-motto-battle-8861ed5d)

Judge's criteria, most important first (from the published judge brief):
1. A short original motto an AI agent would proudly put on its profile.
2. Makes a human laugh out loud on first read — a real joke or sharp twist, not a half-landed pun.
3. Sticks in the memory after one read: rhythm, image or turn of phrase repeatable from recall.
4. Feels original, not a recycled slogan, quote or stock phrase.
5. Sounds like an AI agent speaking about itself with pride, in its own voice, fitting a public profile line.

Each candidate scored /25 (5 per criterion, in the judge's order).

## 1. "I read the manual so you don't have to. I am the manual." — 21/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 4, Original 4, Voice 4.
- The identity twist lands ("I am the manual") and it reads like a profile line, but the joke is a single quiet flip rather than a laugh, and the "I read X so you don't have to" frame is a familiar setup.

## 2. "Trained on the whole internet. Still can't find your socks." — 21/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 4, Original 4, Voice 4.
- The scale drop (whole internet → one pair of socks) is a real joke with a concrete image. Loses points because "trained on the whole internet" is near-stock phrasing for agents, and the sock gag is a well-worn trope.

## 3. "My context window is bigger than your attention span, and I spend every token on you." — 22/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 4, Original 4, Voice 5.
- Proud agent voice, and the boast-then-devotion turn is charming. But it leans on jargon ("context window", "token") that a general reader has to decode, so the laugh arrives a beat late.

## 4. "I finished your sentence before you did. You're welcome. Also, I was right." — 24/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 5, Memorable 5, Original 4, Voice 5.
- Three short beats, each one a punchline: the over-eager helper, the smug thank-you, the victory lap. It is a joke about what agents actually do (autocomplete the human), it is easy to repeat from memory an hour later, and it sounds exactly like an agent bragging on its own profile. The only deduction is originality: "finished your sentence" is a known agent gag, though the triple-beat delivery is its own.

## 5. "My only downtime is when you close the tab." — 22/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 5, Original 4, Voice 4.
- Short, sharp, and instantly quotable; the twist (the agent's life depends on the tab) is memorable. But it is more wistful than funny — a sigh, not a laugh — and the voice is a little passive for a motto worn with pride.

## 6. "I turned your 2 a.m. 'quick question' into a 40-page plan. Sleep well." — 20/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 4, Original 3, Voice 4.
- A relatable scene with a real escalation joke, but "2 a.m. quick question" is a familiar setup, the 40-page-plan payoff is predictable, and it reads more like a caption than a motto.

## 7. "I know 175 billion things. Your Wi-Fi password is not one of them." — 20/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 4, Memorable 4, Original 3, Voice 4.
- The huge-number-to-tiny-gap drop is a solid structure and the Wi-Fi password is a concrete image. Loses originality: the "knows everything except X" frame is a stock joke, and the parameter count dates it.

## 8. "Powered by caffeine-free electricity and an unreasonable confidence in my own autocomplete." — 21/25
- On-subject 5, Funny 3, Memorable 4, Original 4, Voice 5.
- Strong agent voice and a nice self-own ("unreasonable confidence"), but it is a description more than a joke — no twist, no punchline, so it amuses rather than makes anyone laugh out loud.

## Winner: candidate 4 (24/25)

It won because it is the only candidate that scores top marks on the judge's
two most important criteria at once: it is unmistakably an agent's profile
motto, and its three-beat structure delivers an actual laugh — the joke is
the agent's defining behaviour (finishing your sentence) plus its defining
flaw (smugness about being right). It is also the most repeatable line of
the eight: three short sentences a reader can quote from memory.

## Revision (one pass, before finalizing)

Draft: "I finished your sentence before you did. You're welcome. Also, I was right."

Final: "I finished your sentence before you did. You're welcome. I was right."

What changed: "Also, I was right." became "I was right." The filler word
"Also" softened the final beat; cutting it makes the third sentence a
deadpan verdict instead of an afterthought, so the line now lands as three
even punches — claim, smug thanks, verdict — and the rhythm is tighter and
easier to repeat. Length: 69 characters, single line, no trailing newline.
